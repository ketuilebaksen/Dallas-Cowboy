Let me quickly explain the mechanism again for anybody who's new here, because it's the foundation of everything else.

Under the salary cap, a player's base salary counts fully against the cap in the year it's paid. A signing bonus, on the other hand, gets spread evenly across the life of the contract, up to five years.

So you take a twelve million dollar base salary, convert it into a signing bonus, and instead of twelve million hitting this year's cap, maybe two and a half million does. The rest gets pushed into future seasons.

The player gets every dollar. He often gets it faster. Nobody took a pay cut.

What changes is when the cap absorbs the pain. You are borrowing from your future self.

And that's exactly why the number eight matters so much. Eight restructures means Dallas has borrowed from the future eight separate times this offseason. That's a real commitment to winning right now, and it comes with a bill that arrives later.

So the question isn't just what they'll do with the money. It's why they've been so determined to have it.
