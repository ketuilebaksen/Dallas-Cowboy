Let me explain what pass rush win rate actually measures, because it's a much better statistic than sacks for a situation like this.

A sack requires everything to go right. You have to beat your man, the quarterback has to hold the ball, and nobody else can get there first. A great rush that ends with the quarterback throwing it away a half-second early produces no sack at all, even though the rusher did his job perfectly.

Win rate measures the part the rusher actually controls. Did he beat his blocker within a certain window of time? That's it. It strips out luck, coverage, and the quarterback's decisions.

That's why it's useful in a small sample. Sacks are noisy. Wins are closer to a skill measurement.

Now the caveats, because I promised I'd always give them.

Twenty-seven snaps is a very small sample. And this specific metric rewards explosive first steps, which is exactly Lawrence's best trait. So there's a real chance the number is flattering him.

There's also a technical detail worth explaining, because people have brought it up and most fans don't know what it means.

The false step.

When the ball is snapped, a pass rusher wants his first movement to go forward, toward the quarterback. A false step is when that first movement goes backward or sideways instead. It's usually unconscious, a habit from years of playing a certain way.

It sounds tiny. It isn't. That step costs maybe a tenth of a second, and against an NFL tackle, a tenth of a second is the difference between getting to the edge and getting ridden past the quarterback.

The good news is that it's a fixable habit, it's already getting better, and even with that flaw his first step is still explosive. Clean it up completely and the win rate goes up from there.
