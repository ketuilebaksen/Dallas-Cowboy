Let me explain why it's so powerful.

Most cornerbacks in this league are side players. A boundary corner lines up on one side of the formation, every snap, all season. He learns that side. His footwork is drilled to that side. The safety help he expects comes from that side. His whole professional existence is one half of a football field.

So what happens when you stack your two best receivers on the same side?

The defense is forced into a decision, and all of its options are uncomfortable.

Option one. One of your corners travels across the formation to follow his man. Now he's playing on a side he doesn't practice on, with different leverage, different footwork, and help coming from an unfamiliar direction.

Option two. The corners stay where they are, which means one of them is now covering a receiver he didn't prepare for all week.

Option three. Your two best receivers are on one side, so one of them ends up matched against the slot defender, who is almost always the third-best coverage player on the field, not the first.

Every one of those is a worse outcome for the defense than lining up normally. You haven't changed a single play. You've changed who has to guard whom.
