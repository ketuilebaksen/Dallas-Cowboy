But let me tell you why a quad matters more for a receiver than almost any other injury, because the mechanism is specific.

A receiver’s separation is produced in three distinct moments. The release off the line of scrimmage. The burst out of the break. And the late acceleration once he’s past the defender.

All three of those are legs. Specifically quad and hip.

So a quad injury does not take away a receiver’s hands. He can still catch everything thrown to him. What it takes is the half step.

And the half step is the entire job. Half a step is the difference between a first down and an incompletion, between a catch in front of a defender and a pass defended, between a touchdown and a tackle at the three.

Which means a receiver with a quad problem can look completely fine and be substantially less effective, and you will never see it on the broadcast unless you’re watching his first two steps instead of the football.
