Alright. So let’s take the question seriously.

The video wants the offensive coordinator fired. And it names Brian Schottenheimer as the offensive coordinator, which he is not. He is the head coach. The offensive coordinator is Klayton Adams.

But here’s why the confusion accidentally lands on something true.

Brian Schottenheimer calls the plays.

I established that weeks ago when I broke down the Ryan Feder structure, where Feder provides fourth-down recommendations during games and keeps the process organized while the head coach makes the final call. And I explained at the time why that structure exists, because a head coach who also calls the offense is doing two jobs that compete.

Play calling is head down. You’re in a sheet, watching personnel, thinking three snaps ahead. Game management is head up. Clock, timeouts, field position, challenges. And a man cannot do both perfectly in the same moment. That’s not a criticism, it’s a physical limitation.

And I pointed out that this is exactly what failed in Rio. A timeout spent and then not used. A touchback with seven seconds left. Those were management failures rather than play-calling failures, and they happened to a coach who was also calling an offense.

So the real question, properly stated, is not should the offensive coordinator be fired. It’s should the head coach hand the call sheet to his offensive coordinator.

And at two and four with twenty-nine points in two games, that is a legitimate question and I’m going to answer it rather than dodge it.

The case for handing it over is stronger than I’d have said a week ago.

Dallas is two and four. The offense has scored thirteen and sixteen in consecutive games. The head coach is a first-year head coach learning the biggest job in the building. And the organization has already demonstrated it believes in this exact kind of move, because eight days ago they hired Mike Pettine as a senior defensive assistant to support Christian Parker.

That’s the symmetry argument and it’s a good one. If your answer to a struggling defense is to add an experienced voice beside a young coordinator, the parallel answer on offense is to let the actual offensive coordinator coordinate the offense while the head coach runs the football team.

And there’s a third argument that’s the one I find most persuasive. A two-and-four team needs its head coach looking up. Clock management, fourth downs, challenges, timeouts and the trade deadline all get more important as the margin gets thinner, and all of them require a man who isn’t staring at a call sheet.

Now the case against, which I think still wins today.

You do not change a play-caller in the middle of a season unless you are certain, because the install is built in that man’s voice. Every concept, every tag, every situational wrinkle was taught the way he says it, and handing it to somebody else mid-October means a team that has been simplifying all season suddenly re-learns the vocabulary.

Second, this is the same offense that led the National Football League in points per drive four games ago. That is not the résumé of a man who cannot call plays. Whatever has gone wrong in two games, incompetence is not on the list.

Third, and this is the specific one. Schottenheimer himself identified the reason this offense works, and it was familiarity and limited offensive turnover. He said the players have done the work together, Dak understands the plan, and the comfort lets him attack rather than spend every snap sorting through uncertainty.

Changing the play-caller is offensive turnover. You would be removing the exact ingredient the head coach credited for the success.

So my verdict is no, not yet, and the reason is methodological rather than loyal.

You do not solve a problem you have not diagnosed.

If those two low-scoring games were possession starvation and giveaways, then changing the play-caller fixes nothing at all, because the offense was never on the field. And if the efficiency has genuinely collapsed, then you need to know that before you act, not guess at it.

Get the drive chart. Get the yards per drive. Then decide.

And I’ll put a condition on it, because that’s what I do. If Dallas plays Philadelphia on Monday night and the yards per drive are down again with a normal possession count, then I think the head coach should hand the call sheet to Klayton Adams and go run the football team. Two data points is noise. Three with the same signature is a pattern.
