Dallas scored seven points in the competitive portion of a football game.

Seven.

And I want to teach this properly, because garbage-time inflation is the single most deceptive thing in a box score and almost nobody adjusts for it.

When a team leads by two scores late, its defense stops playing the same sport. They rush three and drop eight. They give you the sideline. They play ten yards off, concede the catch, and tackle. The entire objective switches from preventing points to consuming clock.

Points scored against that are real on the scoreboard and nearly meaningless as evidence. They tell you what an offense can do when the other team has stopped contesting.

So the box score says thirteen and the football game says seven. And regular viewers will recognize the shape of this, because it’s the hidden denominator again, except this time the hidden variable isn’t opportunity. It’s context.

Points are not fungible. A point scored against a defense playing to win is worth more than a point scored against a defense playing the clock, and any statistic that treats them identically is lying to you politely.
