Let me explain those three coverages in plain language, because the names are intimidating and the ideas are simple.

Cover one is man coverage with one safety deep in the middle. Every defender has a person. One man cleans up behind them.

Cover three divides the deep part of the field into three equal pieces. Two cornerbacks take the outside thirds, one safety takes the middle third, and four defenders handle the shorter areas underneath. Everybody has a zone, not a man.

Quarters divides the deep field into four pieces instead of three, which takes away deep throws better but leaves fewer bodies underneath and requires much more communication, because defenders have to read route combinations together and decide in real time who takes whom.

Now, why would a defensive coordinator who just acquired a man-coverage cornerback go to more cover three?

Because cover three is the coverage everybody in football knows at a basic level.
