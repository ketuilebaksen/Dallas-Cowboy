And let me explain why that jump is genuinely hard, because I think fans underestimate it.

A position coach spends his week in a very controlled world. He watches film of the opponent's receivers, he teaches his corners technique and leverage, he prepares them for what's coming. His work is done before kickoff.

A defensive coordinator calling plays lives in a completely different world on Sunday. He's getting information from coaches in the booth upstairs, he's watching tendencies develop in real time, he has maybe fifteen or twenty seconds between snaps to pick a call from a menu of dozens, and he has to anticipate what the offensive coordinator across the field is about to do to him.

And the offensive coordinator is doing the same thing in reverse. It's a chess match where both players move at the same time and neither can see the other's board.

Some brilliant teachers never become great play-callers. Some great play-callers were ordinary position coaches. They're related skills, but they are not the same skill.

So the honest question isn't whether Christian Parker knows defense. He clearly does. It's whether he can make the in-game adjustment when the opponent finds something. Week One said not yet. Today is the second data point.
