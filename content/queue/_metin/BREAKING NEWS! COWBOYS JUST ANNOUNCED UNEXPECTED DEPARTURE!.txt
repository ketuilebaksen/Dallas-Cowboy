Let me walk you through the mechanics, because I think a lot of people are throwing the phrase franchise tag around without really understanding what it does and what it costs.

The franchise tag is a one-year mechanism. It lets a team keep a player who would otherwise become an unrestricted free agent, and the price is set by formula, not by negotiation. For a wide receiver, that number is tied to the average of the top salaries at the position across the league.

So the team doesn't get to decide what the tag costs. The market decides. The team only decides whether to use it.

And there are two versions. The exclusive tag, which means the player cannot negotiate with anybody else at all. And the non-exclusive tag, which means other teams can make an offer, but if they sign him, they owe the Cowboys two first-round picks.

Two first-round picks. That's the tollbooth. In practice, almost nobody pays it. Which is why the non-exclusive tag functions as a wall even though it technically has a door in it.

Now here is the part people miss, and it's the most important part.

The tag is not a solution. It's a delay.

You tag a guy, you buy yourself one season. And at the end of that season, you're right back in the same room having the exact same conversation, except now he's a year older, he's had another year of production, and if you want to tag him again, the price goes up significantly. Tag him a third time and it becomes genuinely punitive.

So the tag is a bridge. It's what you use when you believe a long-term deal is coming but you need more time to structure it, or when you want one more year of evidence before committing enormous guaranteed money.

It is not what you use when you've decided a player is your future. If you've decided that, you sign him.
