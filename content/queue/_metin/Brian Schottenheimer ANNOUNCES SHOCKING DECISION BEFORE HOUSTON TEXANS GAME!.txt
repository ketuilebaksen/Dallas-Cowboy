Now, since I just spent a minute complaining that this video ignored the football game, let me actually give you the preview it should have been, because there’s a layer to Lambeau on Sunday night that I haven’t heard anybody mention and it genuinely worries me.

It’s the noise.

And not for the reason you think.

Everybody knows crowd noise is a factor at Lambeau Field at night. But most people get the mechanics backwards, so let me be precise, because the direction matters enormously here.

Crowd noise is asymmetric by design. A home crowd is loud when the visiting offense has the football and goes quiet when its own offense is working, because the fans want their quarterback to be able to run his operation.

Which means Lambeau’s noise does essentially nothing for the Dallas defense. It is aimed entirely at the Dallas offense.

And here’s why that lands on this particular Dallas team at this particular moment.

Communication has been the defining problem of this season. Week One was nine of eleven third downs converted, with the head coach describing basic communication breakdowns. Dallas spent a month simplifying. They built green-dot redundancy on defense specifically because they’d been burned by not having it. And the single most encouraging thing Schottenheimer has said all year is that communication has improved since Week One.

But that’s the defense. On Sunday night the noise is pointed at the offense.

And the first game they get together is in the loudest building in professional football, at night, where they cannot hear each other speak.

That’s the tension, and it’s a real one. Offensive line communication is not atmosphere. It’s specific verbal work on every single snap. Which way is the slide going. Who has the twist. Who picks up the late blitzer. Is the center changing the call. That all happens out loud, in about four seconds, before the ball moves.

Take away the ability to talk and a chain of five becomes five individuals, which is exactly the thing I told you a line cannot be.

Now, the answer exists and it’s silent communication. Hand signals, taps, rehearsed rules, pre-snap looks. Good lines have it and it works. But it works best when the pair has played four hundred snaps together, not four.
