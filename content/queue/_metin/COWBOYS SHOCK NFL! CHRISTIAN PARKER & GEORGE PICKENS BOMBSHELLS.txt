An edge rusher and an off-ball linebacker look at football in completely different ways.

An edge rusher's eyes go to one place. The man in front of him and the quarterback behind him. His job is to attack, to win a one-on-one, to get home. It's aggressive and it's simple in the best sense.

A middle linebacker's eyes have to be everywhere. He reads the guards to diagnose run or pass. He finds the back. He finds the tight end. He carries somebody up the seam or passes him off to a safety, all while the offense is actively trying to make him look in the wrong place.

Converting from the first job to the second is one of the hardest transitions in football. You are asking a player whose instincts say go to instead play with patience and discipline, reading before reacting.

That's the transition Barham is in the middle of. And on Sunday night he was doing it live, on national television, in his first professional game.
