Next, the cornerback who has been starting opposite DaRon Bland was ruled out almost immediately with a hamstring injury.

And let me explain why hamstrings are so frustrating for defensive backs specifically, because this is the position where that injury is at its worst.

A cornerback's entire job is sprinting and then stopping. Backpedal, plant, drive on the ball, turn and run with a receiver who knows where he's going. Every one of those movements loads the hamstring violently.

A lineman can sometimes play through a mild hamstring strain. A cornerback really can't, because the first time he has to open his hips and run with a receiver at full speed, he's either going to get beaten or tear it further.

And the recurrence risk is the real enemy. Bring a defensive back back one week too early, and a two-week injury can turn into a six-week one. That's why teams tend to be conservative, and why I'd be surprised to see him in Rio.

So what should Dallas do?

The most interesting debate this week is about Caleb Downs.

There's a strong argument out there that Dallas should move Downs back to free safety. He's a safety by background. He's smart, he communicates, and he'd immediately be the best option at the position.

And against Washington, it didn't happen. Downs played only one or two snaps at safety, and those seemed to come when there simply weren't receivers for him to cover in the slot. So the staff has made its preference pretty clear. They want him at nickel.

Let me give you both sides of that, because it's a genuinely hard call.

The case for moving him is simple. You have more players who can play nickel than you have safeties you trust. Put your best available player at the position where you're thinnest.

The case against is the one we laid out last week. Downs' value comes from being near the line of scrimmage, seeing plays develop and directing traffic. And with a rookie on the green dot, Downs has been the second communicator right next to him. Move him to deep safety and you lose that, in the exact area where this defense broke down in Week One.

And here's the wrinkle that makes this week different.

Against a normal quarterback, I'd lean toward keeping Downs at nickel. But against Lamar Jackson, the free safety job changes.

When a quarterback can extend plays with his legs, receivers keep running. A play that should last three seconds lasts six, and the deep part of the field opens up late. The free safety is the player who has to stay disciplined in that moment, keep his eyes right, and not get pulled toward the line when the quarterback breaks the pocket.

That's a demanding, veteran job. And putting an emergency option there against Lamar Jackson is a real risk.

So honestly, I'm less sure this week than I was last week. If P.J. Locke is out for a while, I think the Downs-to-safety conversation stops being a fan theory and becomes a real option the staff has to consider.

The other paths are the usual ones. Promote somebody from the practice squad. Sign a veteran safety off the street who knows how to line up and communicate. Or explore a trade.
