Let me explain why avoiding injured reserve actually matters, because it's more than a technicality.

Putting a player on IR means he must miss a minimum of four games. Four. It's a hard floor, not an estimate. So a team only does it when it's certain the absence will be that long, because the moment you make that move, you've lost that player for a month even if he heals in three weeks.

Keeping a player off IR is a team saying, we think there's a real chance he's back before four games are up, and we want that option.

But here's the catch, and it's the part I want you to hold onto.

Not going on injured reserve does not mean available. Even in the most optimistic version of this, both of these players are out for the immediate future. That includes Baltimore, and quite possibly the week after.

Good news on paper does not become good news on the field for at least a couple of weeks.

And now let me be straight with you about the diagnosis itself, because I think the optimism deserves a little scrutiny.

The plantar fascia is a thick band of tissue running along the bottom of your foot, from the heel to the toes. It's essentially the arch support your body was born with. Every time you push off, it stretches and recoils and helps generate force.

Now think about what a defensive back does for a living. Backpedal, plant hard, drive forward. Turn and run. Every one of those movements is a violent push-off through the bottom of the foot.

A torn plantar fascia is not a minor injury for a defensive back. It's about as position-specific a problem as you can get.

Now, I want to be fair, because there's a wrinkle here that cuts the other way, and it's genuinely interesting.

Some players with a full tear actually return faster than players dealing with chronic plantar fasciitis, because the constant pain from the inflamed tissue is what was limiting them, and once it tears, that source of pain is gone. It's a strange thing, but it happens.

So it's possible the medical staff looked at the imaging and saw a version of this that heals quicker than the words sound. That may be exactly why they're not talking about injured reserve.

But I'd plan for weeks, not days. And I'd be stunned to see him in Rio.

Now, the obvious question. Why not just move somebody?

Remember what actually broke this defense in Week One. It wasn't talent. It was communication. Six new defensive starters, players who had never taken a regular season snap together, and a string of blown assignments before the snap.

Now imagine you respond to an injury crisis by moving three players to positions they haven't practiced at. You've solved a talent problem by creating a brand new communication problem, in the exact area where this team already broke once.

That's the coaching staff's logic, and it's not crazy.
