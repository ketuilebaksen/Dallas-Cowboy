Three things failed on Sunday night, and here's the part people miss. They are not three separate problems. They're one problem showing up in three places.

Let me show you how those connect, because once you see it you can't unsee it.

It starts up front. When a pass rush doesn't get home, the quarterback gets extra time. Not a lot. Half a second, a second. But a defensive back's assignment is only designed to hold for so long, because the scheme assumes the ball is coming out.

Give a quarterback an extra beat and receivers finish their routes, find the soft spots in a zone, and break open in exactly the places where the coverage is thinnest.

So now your secondary looks out of position. And some of that really is a secondary problem. But a meaningful chunk of it is a pass rush problem wearing a secondary costume.

Then the linebackers. When a defense is getting picked apart, players start guessing. They jump routes they shouldn't. They arrive at a tackle a half-step early and out of control instead of squared up and balanced. That's how you get missed tackles from guys who tackle just fine in practice.

Panic is contagious on a defense, and it shows up as poor technique.

So which is the root cause? I'd argue it's the pass rush, because pressure is the one thing that covers for everything else. A quarterback who has to throw in two seconds cannot exploit a blown coverage, because the receiver hasn't gotten there yet.

When a defense is making mental errors, the answer is almost never more complexity. It's less. Give them fewer checks. Fewer disguises.
