When a player is going to miss a month, a team has to decide whether to put him on injured reserve.

Here's the rule. Once you place a player on injured reserve during the season, he must miss a minimum of four games. Not four weeks. Four games. There is no early return, no matter how well he heals.

So the calculation goes like this. If you genuinely believe a man is out for a month anyway, putting him on injured reserve costs you nothing and buys you a roster spot to go sign somebody who can actually play on Sunday.

But if there's a chance he comes back in three weeks, injured reserve turns three into four, and you've made your own injury worse with paperwork.

That's why teams sit on these decisions. And it's why the words a month or even longer matter so much. A month or longer is exactly the description that pushes a front office toward injured reserve, because the downside disappears.
