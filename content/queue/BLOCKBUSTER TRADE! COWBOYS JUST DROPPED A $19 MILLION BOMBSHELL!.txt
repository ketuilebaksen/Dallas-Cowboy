Now let me stop and explain what a restructure actually is, because this gets misunderstood constantly and understanding it will change how you read every cap story for the rest of your life.

Here's the rule. Under the salary cap, a player's base salary counts entirely against the cap in the year it's paid. But a signing bonus gets spread evenly across the life of the contract, up to five years.

So if a player has a twelve million dollar base salary this season, that's twelve million on this year's cap. But if you convert that same twelve million into a signing bonus on a five-year deal, suddenly it's only counting about two point four million this year, with the rest pushed into future seasons.

You just freed up nearly ten million dollars today. The player gets the exact same money. He might even get it faster, since signing bonuses are typically paid up front.
