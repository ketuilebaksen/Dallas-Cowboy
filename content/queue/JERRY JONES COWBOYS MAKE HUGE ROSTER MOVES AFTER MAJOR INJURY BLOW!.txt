The injured reserve decision on Jalen Thompson.

Here's the rule, and it is the single most misunderstood rule in roster management.

When you place a player on injured reserve during the regular season, he must miss a minimum of four games. Not four weeks. Four games. And there is no early return, no matter how well he heals, no matter how good he looks in practice.

So the calculation a front office runs is simple. If you genuinely believe a man will be out for a month anyway, then putting him on injured reserve costs you nothing you were going to use. And in exchange, you get a roster spot back, which you can spend on somebody who can actually play on Sunday.

But if there's a real chance he's back in three weeks, injured reserve turns three into four. You've made your own injury worse with paperwork.

That's why teams sit on these decisions for days.

And now look at the specific language. A month, possibly longer. That is exactly the phrase that makes the decision easy, because the downside disappears.

The practice squad.

Two rules you need.

Rule one. Teams can elevate practice squad players to the game day roster, up to three times each per season, without signing them to the fifty-three. That's the magic of it. An elevation does not cost you a roster spot. The player suits up, plays in the game, and goes back to the practice squad on Monday.

Rule two. Teams may protect only four practice squad players per week from being signed away by other clubs. Everybody else is available to any team in the league that's willing to put them on its active roster.

So elevations are the cheapest tool in football. No roster spot, no contract commitment, and the player already knows your building because he practices with you every day and runs the scout team.

There are four levers available to any team, and they are not equally expensive.

Lever one is the snap count. It is completely free. You take snaps from a man who isn't producing and give them to a man who might. It costs no picks, no money, and no roster spots.

Lever two is the practice squad. Almost free. Three elevations per player, no roster spot.

Lever three is the street free agent. Cheap. Costs a roster spot and a modest contract.

Lever four is the trade. Expensive. Costs draft capital, and often money too.

A well-run front office pulls those levers in that order. You try the free thing first and the expensive thing last.
