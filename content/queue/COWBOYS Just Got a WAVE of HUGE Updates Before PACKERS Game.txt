Now the most interesting football development of the week, and nobody is going to explain this properly, so let me do it.

Jaishawn Barham was seen working with the edge rushers this week.

Barham is the rookie middle linebacker. He carries the green dot. He relays the defensive calls. And he was with the edge group.

Now, three things about that, and the first one is the key that unlocks it.

Number one. In this defense, that is a much smaller move than it sounds.

Dallas runs a three-four front. Schottenheimer confirmed it himself when he explained the Pettine hire. And in a three-four, the outside linebackers are the edge rushers. Same position group, same room, same job title.

So Barham working with the edge rushers is not a dramatic conversion from one side of the ball to another. It’s a lateral move from inside linebacker to outside linebacker, inside the same front, in a scheme where both spots are called linebacker.

In a four-three defense that would be a big deal, because you’d be turning a linebacker into a defensive lineman. In a three-four it’s a Tuesday.

Number two, and this is why I think it’s smart. It takes his strength and removes his weakness.

Everything we know about Barham from the head coach points the same direction. His downhill aggression is a strength and nobody wants to coach it out of him. And his coverage responsibilities are new, he has to count receiving threats from the outside inward on every snap, and play-action can still pull him forward.

So he’s an aggressive attacker who’s still learning to cover and who bites on fakes.

Now look at what an outside linebacker in a three-four does. He sets the edge against the run and rushes the passer. Attack, attack, attack. Almost no coverage. And play-action barely matters, because you’re going forward either way.

That’s not a reassignment. That’s a man being moved to the job he already does well.

And it lands exactly on the diagnosis I gave you this week. I said the run defense failure in this front is that nobody is holding the edge, and that the men responsible for holding it are also the men being asked to chase sacks. Putting your most aggressive, most physical young linebacker on that edge is a direct response to that.

And it’s the free lever. Snap counts. No pick, no money, no roster spot. Exactly the move I said to watch for.

Number three, the cost. And this is the part that makes it genuinely interesting.

If Barham moves out to the edge, who relays the defensive calls?

Because the green dot is on his helmet. And you can’t have your communication hub chasing an offensive tackle up the field while eleven men wait for the call.

And here’s where something clicks that I covered weeks ago and didn’t fully appreciate.

Schottenheimer told us Dallas has trained multiple defenders to handle the green dot and relay calls. And he specifically admitted they didn’t have enough alternatives prepared last year when Jack Sanborn wasn’t available.

Everybody read that as injury insurance. I read it as insurance against the rookie wall. And I think today we learn there was a third purpose, which is positional flexibility.

If the green dot can move, then Barham can move. And that redundancy, built in the summer for one reason, is now paying off for a reason nobody stated.

That’s a staff that built an option and is now cashing it. I’ll give credit for that.
