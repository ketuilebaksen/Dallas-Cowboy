Now, for anybody who isn't deep in offensive line terminology, let me quickly explain what people mean by gap scheme and counter, because that's the exact language Schottenheimer used.

A gap scheme, in the simplest terms, is a run play built around pulling an offensive guard across the formation to lead the way and create a running lane. Instead of everybody blocking the man in front of them, you're moving a lineman laterally to attack a specific gap with a numbers advantage.

Counter plays work off a similar principle, but with misdirection built in. The running back takes a step in one direction to get the defense flowing that way, and then the play actually hits back the other direction, behind pulling linemen.

Here's why that matters for a specific type of back.

Both concepts depend heavily on the running back's patience and vision. You cannot just hit the hole as fast as possible, because the hole doesn't exist yet. You have to let the blocking develop in front of you, read where it's going, and then accelerate.

Impatient backs are useless in these schemes. They run into the backs of their own linemen.

Backs with vision and buildup speed thrive in them. Which is exactly the profile Demercado has, and exactly the system Klayton Adams is running.

That's not a coincidence. That's a front office matching a player to a scheme on purpose.
