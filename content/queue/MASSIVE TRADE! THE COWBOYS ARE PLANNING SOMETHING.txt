Thursday night, Dallas restructured the contracts of three players. Tight end Jake Ferguson. Cornerback DaRon Bland. Defensive tackle Kenny Clark. That generated just over nineteen million dollars in new cap space.

Let me be precise about the mechanics, because this gets misunderstood constantly.

This was not a pay cut. None of these three men are making less money.

What happened is that their 2026 base salaries were converted into signing bonuses. And under the cap rules, signing bonuses get prorated across the length of a contract, while base salary counts entirely in the year it's paid.

So the hit that was sitting on the 2026 books gets spread across multiple years. The player still gets every dollar he was owed, often faster. The team gets relief now, at the cost of slightly higher numbers later.

Here's the breakdown. Clark's eight point eight million base salary was converted. Bland's twelve million was converted. Ferguson's seven point two five million was converted.

And the back-end cost is genuinely modest. Clark's 2027 number goes up about one and a half million. Bland's rises roughly two million over the remainder of his deal. Ferguson's goes up about one point two million.
