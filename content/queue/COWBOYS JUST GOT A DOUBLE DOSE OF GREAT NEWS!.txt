Managing an injury means holding it steady. Playing through one means spending it.

Now the three names for one problem, because this is the cleanest example of a thing I’ve been explaining for a month.

The official injury report lists an ankle. Schottenheimer said after the Colts game that it involves his heel. Porter describes the whole foot.

Three different words for one body part.

And the video’s conclusion is exactly right. It is one area of the body and different people are naming different parts of it.

So the principle, and it’s a media-literacy point rather than a medical one.

An injury designation is a filing, not a diagnosis.

A team files a body part on a form because the rules require a body part. The word has to be one of a short list, and it has to be consistent, and it cannot be a paragraph. So ankle appears on the report because ankle is the nearest available box, and then a coach says heel because that’s where the pain is, and then the player says the whole foot is jacked up because that’s what it feels like from inside.

None of those three people is lying. The form just isn’t built to describe a foot.

Which is why you should never read an injury report as a medical document, and why an ankle on a form can be a fortnight of a man’s season.

Now the arithmetic of Sunday, because the video does something I want to praise and then extend.

Which brings me to the detail that decides Porter’s weekend, and it’s the best piece of football explanation in the video.

Schottenheimer explained last week that when you’re a corner, foot problems can be tricky. A cornerback has to plant, turn and run with a receiver on every snap.

That’s exactly it, and I made the same argument a week ago about a different player with a foot injury, so let me restate the mechanism.

A cornerback’s entire job is force through the foot. The backpedal, the transition where he flips his hips and turns to run, the plant that redirects him when a receiver breaks. All of it, at speed, repeated forty times, against a man paid to make him do it twice in one second.

So a foot that won’t take a hard plant doesn’t make a corner slower in a straight line. It makes him late out of every break.

Which is why Porter wanted to test running and cutting on Friday, and why running on Thursday is progress rather than an answer. Running is force in one direction. Cutting is force in a direction you didn’t plan on, and that’s the one the job requires.

And it’s why last week’s pattern is the one to remember. A solid practice on Friday and soreness on Saturday. The test isn’t whether he can do it. It’s whether he can do it twice.
