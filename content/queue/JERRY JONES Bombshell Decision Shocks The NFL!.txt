Alright. Now the Dallas side, and this is where the real analysis lives, because what happened here is a specific roster mechanic that almost nobody understands and I’ve explained before.

Philadelphia signed Joe Milton off the Dallas practice squad.

Not traded for. Not claimed on waivers. Signed.

And here’s how that works, because it’s one of the strangest rules in professional football.

A practice squad player is not under contract to your team in the way an active roster player is. Any other team in the league can sign him to their active roster at any time, and your team cannot stop it. You don’t get compensation. You don’t get a phone call. You get a notification.

The only protection is the protection list. Each week, a team can designate four practice squad players as protected, and those four cannot be signed away. Everybody else is available to anybody.

A backup quarterback can have two possible jobs, and they’re mutually exclusive.

Job one is to be ready to win games you’d otherwise lose. That requires a veteran who knows the system cold and needs zero developmental snaps.

Job two is to develop into a future starter. That requires practice repetitions, first-team work, and meaningful snaps.

And I said a contending team cannot pay for job two, because the currency of development is snaps and those snaps belong to the starter. So a high-upside young quarterback behind a healthy franchise quarterback gets neither thing. He doesn’t develop, because he doesn’t play.
